💡 Key Concepts

  • A supernode is used when an ideal voltage source directly connects two non-reference nodes.
  • The voltage source fixes VB − VC, but its current is not known from Ohm's law. That is why ordinary nodal analysis cannot write a conductance term through the source itself.
  • Enclose B, C and the voltage source inside one boundary, then apply KCL to currents crossing that boundary.
  • The second equation is the source constraint: VB − VC = Eₛ.
  • After VB and VC are solved, the current through the ideal voltage source follows from KCL at either internal node.
  • A negative branch current simply means the physical current is opposite to the chosen reference arrow; the animation reverses automatically.

Σ Supernode Equations

(VB − E₁)/R₁ + VB/R₃ + (VC − E₂)/R₂ + VC/R₄ = 0

VB − VC = Eₛ

I₁ = (E₁ − VB)/R₁

I₂ = (E₂ − VC)/R₂

I₃ = VB/R₃ · I₄ = VC/R₄

Iₛ = I₁ − I₃ = I₄ − I₂

Model assumptions: ideal DC voltage sources, ideal wires, ohmic resistors and steady state. Positive Eₛ means B is Eₛ volts above C. Positive Iₛ is B → C.
