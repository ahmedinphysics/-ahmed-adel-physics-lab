💡 Key Concepts

  • Kirchhoff's Current Law is a direct consequence of conservation of electric charge.
  • At junction J₁, the incoming current splits: I = I₁ + I₂.
  • At junction J₂, the two branch currents recombine: I₁ + I₂ = I.
  • Current is not “used up” by a resistor. The branch resistance determines how much current takes that path.
  • Increasing R₁ reduces I₁. With an ideal voltage source, I₂ is unchanged because the branch voltage remains V.
  • Opening a branch makes its current zero; the remaining closed branch carries all of the total current.
