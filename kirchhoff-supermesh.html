💡 Key Concepts

  • A supermesh is used when an ideal current source lies on the branch shared by two meshes.
  • The ideal current source fixes its branch current, but its voltage is whatever the rest of the circuit requires. That is why ordinary mesh KVL cannot cross that branch directly.
  • We temporarily exclude the shared current-source branch and write one KVL equation around the outer perimeter of both meshes.
  • The missing equation comes from the current source itself: with the arrows used here, J₁ − J₂ = Iₛ.
  • A negative J₁ or J₂ means the actual current is opposite to the chosen clockwise reference. The animation reverses automatically.
  • An ideal current source can either deliver or absorb power depending on the voltage that develops across it and the direction of its current.

Σ Supermesh Equations

E₁ − J₁R₁ − J₂R₂ − E₂ = 0

J₁ − J₂ = Iₛ

J₁ = (E₁ − E₂ + IₛR₂) / (R₁ + R₂)

J₂ = (E₁ − E₂ − IₛR₁) / (R₁ + R₂)

VB = E₁ − J₁R₁ = E₂ + J₂R₂

Pcurrent source, absorbed = VBDIₛ

Model assumptions: ideal DC voltage sources, one ideal DC current source, ideal wires, ohmic resistors and steady state. Positive Iₛ is downward B → D. Positive mesh currents J₁ and J₂ are clockwise.
